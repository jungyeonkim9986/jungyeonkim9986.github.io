Jungyeon Kim

Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at Nanyang Technological University, Singapore

I work on industrial AI for flexible manufacturing. My research follows a product from how it is designed to how its production is designed, and asks what changes when the two are designed together: product-production co-design.

In the first years of my Ph.D. I applied machine learning and reinforcement learning to additive manufacturing. My current work has moved beyond that application to robotics, product design and product-production co-design, with new papers in preparation.

Before starting my Ph.D. in 2024, I completed a B.Eng. (Honours) in Mechanical Engineering at NTU as an NTU President Research Scholar, and interned at Samsung Electronics, GlobalFoundries and Hyundai Motor Company.

Research

Product design

How a product is structured decides how flexibly it can be made. I study product architecture, including software-defined products whose functions are set in software rather than fixed in hardware.

Papers: software-defined product architecture

Production design

I work on how factories are configured and run so they can switch between products: software-defined factories, robotics, and reinforcement learning that decides what a line builds next and which machine to repair first.

Papers: software-defined factory, scheduling and repair

Product-production co-design

Design choices change what production has to do, and production limits what can be designed. I develop AI methods that treat the product and the system that makes it as one design problem.

Papers: design complexity and scheduling

Earlier application: additive manufacturing. In the first years of my Ph.D. I applied data-efficient machine learning and deep reinforcement learning to metal additive manufacturing. Papers: bead shape, bulk metallic glass, sintering and aging, melt pool depth.
